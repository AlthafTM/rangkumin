# Study-note structure and readability

## Summary

Preserve the conceptual spine: definitions needed for use, important rules and formulas, symbol meanings, applicability conditions, representative procedures, distinctions, and memory facts likely to be assessed. Do not omit non-calculation material just because equations are easier to extract.

Follow the requested week/chapter grouping. Where one topic is the focus, use earlier topics as a compact foundation and link the concepts when needed. Avoid repeating the entire earlier summary.

A useful topic sequence is: concrete example, brief meaning, rule/formula, worked example, memory points. Adjust it to the user's request. If examples must appear at the end, put them there; if examples from the slides are to be appended under existing text, add them without rewriting the preserved explanation.

Explain “what is X?” through a small example where possible. Keep explanatory prose short, but do not skip mathematical transformations. Use tables only for genuinely comparable items. Use simple graphs or diagrams where relationships are otherwise hard to see.

## Worked examples

Reproduce the relevant problem/code accurately, cite its source location unobtrusively, state the rule, substitute, simplify step by step, and identify the final answer. Label newly authored problems as additional examples. Choose them to demonstrate a different condition rather than changing only a number when the user wants conceptual variety.

Avoid paragraphs explaining every elementary arithmetic step. Add explanation where a step changes the counting model, domain, probability assumption, summation bounds, or algorithm behavior.

## Memory points and patterns

Use compact bullets for high-value facts, contrast pairs, common traps, and recognition cues. Keep examples next to the relevant facts. Tips must describe when a method works, not just a shortcut to memorize.

Examples: inclusive versus exclusive bounds; uniform versus weighted averages; required versus optional use-case behavior; stack LIFO versus queue FIFO. Do not add unrelated lists merely to make a guide look comprehensive.

## Cheatsheet

Create a separate artifact when requested. Use the pattern:

| Situation | Formula/rule | Condition | Tiny example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recognizable trigger | Readable notation | Assumption or boundary | One substitution/result |

Use a compact layout rather than shrinking the font until unreadable. Include alternate cases only when they change how the rule is used. Keep derivations in the full notes. For power sums, show the sigma expression alongside its closed form, not only a label such as “S2.”

## Revision behavior

Treat feedback as a targeted correction. Preserve approved material, examples, numbering, language, and format unless the user asks to change them or a correction requires it. Recheck affected cross-references and final results. Do not respond to “add this below” by replacing the earlier text.
